## 5. The film: one coherent 60-second story

Audience: professionals who want to stay present in meetings and get useful follow-through afterward. Avoid a tour of developer settings or a list of model/vendor names.

Use this starting timeline. Refine wording for the actual evidence, but keep total runtime exactly 60 seconds and preserve the causal sequence.

| Time | Moment | Show, rather than explain |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 s | Stay in the conversation. | A quiet, synthetic meeting scene and genuine Métis notes. Establish the note-taking product immediately. No long logo intro. |
| 6–13 s | Notes become useful. | A legible decision and action item form from permitted fixture input. Keep authentic UI, restrained emphasis and real processing semantics. |
| 13–21 s | One key away. | The actual shortcut summons the existing Métis surface while the meeting continues. Ask a typed contextual question; a source-linked response appears. |
| 21–30 s | Put it to work. | A specifically requested follow-up draft is prepared in the correct target. Show truthful progress and verified output, not an automatic send or fake cursor. |
| 30–38 s | Keep what matters. | Explicitly end the meeting; review and approve one decision for memory. Show actual memory status, never a fake instantaneous success. |
| 38–49 s | Pick up with context. | Clearly mark “Later · new session.” Ask what was agreed. Retrieve approved Hindsight memory with its source. A brief real voice reply can occur outside the call. |
| 49–55 s | Still your call. | Show the relevant memory/source control and one useful refinement or interruption. UI truth, not a disconnected privacy slogan. |
| 55–60 s | Métis. | A strong, settled brand ending. Suggested line: “Stay present. Carry it forward.” Use only the real approved CTA and availability. |

If a target capability is not verified, change the copy/sequence or label the concept. Never cut away precisely where authorization or verification would disprove the promise.

**Primary visual payoff:** follow one approved decision from the real meeting record into later source-backed assistance. Keep its wording, source identity and target consistent. The viewer should understand why continuity matters without seeing a database diagram.

Time jumps and condensed waits need clear editorial treatment. Do not turn an edited processing sequence into an apparent real-time speed benchmark. Preserve at least one uninterrupted interaction with an honest end state when footage permits.

## 6. Art direction and motion

Read actual Métis brand/design files. Preserve its logo, approved typography, color roles, mesh/orb treatment and product geometry. Where Mantu attribution is required, use the approved asset. Do not substitute Apple branding or invented brand rules.

Use disciplined negative space, careful lighting, crisp surfaces, large readable copy and limited controlled color. Composition can alternate quiet contrast treatments; the app itself must retain its real theme unless a theme change is explicitly demonstrated.

Camera movement directs attention to a meaningful target. Use motivated close-ups, shared-element continuity, precise masking and restrained acceleration/deceleration. Do not rotate the whole UI randomly, apply transitions to every word, or turn the film into kinetic PowerPoint.

Keep app-state motion separate from film decoration: a recording glow must reflect recording, an output orb must follow actual speech playback, and a success animation must follow success evidence—not the music beat.

No generic particle fields, stock robots, gratuitous neural networks, fake chat walls, unreadable floating cards, strobing, repeated bass-drop zooms, or borrowed Apple/Notchview footage/music. Use only authorized assets. Do not ship font binaries with the deliverable.

Readable copy gets real settled screen time, not just entrance/exit time. Favor one main idea and a short line per shot. Never shrink important product text until it becomes decorative texture. Test on a phone-sized preview.

Before production, compare three short hook concepts on product clarity, specificity, emotion and evidence. Select one and proceed; do not render three complete films unnecessarily.
